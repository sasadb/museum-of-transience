THE MUSEUM OF TRANSIENCE

The Museum of Transience collects what does not stay.
Home, for me now, is made of things that do not last: Royco sachets that run out, train tickets good for one ride, twelve songs for the twelve months I could not go back.
I keep rebuilding Indonesia in a small one-room, one meal at a time, and each time, it is a little different.
For now, I keep those things here.
Take your time.

ROOM I

Things That Carry Taste & Good Fortune

For ingredients, scents, and rituals that make an unfamiliar room feel like home.

OBJECT #01 • AI-RECONSTRUCTED PAINTING, 2026

Bamboe Soto Ayam

Soto Without the Warung

Back home, I almost never cooked soto. It was always nearby, warm and filling, from a roadside stall to a five-star hotel. In Korea, I have not found Indonesian restaurants selling it, and the seasoning is easy enough, so I had to make it myself. When late autumn arrives, it is what I cook again and again. The taste is not quite the same. There are no condiments, no fried snacks, no warung around the corner. Still, it brings something back.

OBJECT #02 • AI-RECONSTRUCTED PAINTING, 2026

Royco

Indonesian in a Sachet

"Some things taste like home because they are temporary."

I think of Royco as the magic MSG of Indonesian cooking. Unlike seasonings from elsewhere, it makes whatever I cook taste distinctly Indonesian. I use it in almost every dish. Not every ingredient I grew up with is easy to find in Korea, but this little sachet is usually within reach. It is ordinary, inexpensive, and quickly used up. Maybe that is part of why I notice it. Something so temporary can still make a meal taste like somewhere I know.

OBJECT #03 • AI-RECONSTRUCTED PAINTING, 2026

Bumbu Marinasi Tempe/Ayam/Ikan

Salty, Savory, Golden

This all-purpose marinade works on tempeh, chicken, or fish, but I like it best with fried tempeh. The soybeans soften as they marinate, the frying turns them golden brown, and the smell of coriander fills the kitchen. Salty, savory, and, in my honest opinion, delicious. It is not an elaborate recipe or a special occasion. It is simply one of the things I make when I want food to taste familiar. Sometimes that is enough.

OBJECT #04 • AI-RECONSTRUCTED PAINTING, 2026

Lada (Indonesian Pepper)

Pepper I Carry With Me

I did not think much about Indonesian spices until I left Indonesia. After living abroad, I began noticing how different the pepper I found elsewhere tasted. So I started carrying Indonesian spices with me. This small packet of pepper is one of them. It follows me into my cooking, meeting Royco and whatever ingredients I can find here. Somehow, the taste brings something back—not exactly Indonesia itself, but a version of it I can still make in my own kitchen.

OBJECT #05 • AI-RECONSTRUCTED PAINTING, 2026

Teh (Indonesian Tea)

Tea for Every Ailment

To me, warm tea used to cure almost everything. Its jasmine scent also brings back my grandmother's village, where tea was rarely sweetened. I used to resent having to fetch sugar. Now, I prefer mine unsweetened. The memory changed somewhere along the way. What once felt like an ordinary inconvenience has become something I remember with affection. Perhaps distance does that: it makes small things noticeable only after they are no longer part of everyday life.

OBJECT #06 • 3D SCAN, 2026

Crusita Es Teh Perfume

Uniquely Indonesian, Quietly So

I think of this perfume as quietly Indonesian. Its tea and jasmine notes remind me of the kitchen shelf, of drinks served at home, of a scent I can recognize without needing to name it. It does not smell like Indonesia in an obvious way. There are no loud spices or heavy sweetness. Instead, something about it feels familiar. With these notes, memories of home return softly, without asking to be noticed. It smells like home without being loud about it.

OBJECT #07 • 3D SCAN, 2026

Bugeo (Dried Pollack Craft)

Good Luck for a New Room

Every Korean class came with a cultural activity. One day, we made bugeo. Seonsaengnim told us that Koreans usually place it in a new home when moving, for good luck. Now I have moved into a new one-room, and I display mine there for the same reason. It sits in my room, quietly becoming part of it. I did not grow up with this custom. Yet here it is, in my room.

OBJECT #08 • 3D SCAN, 2026

Home of Humans Reed Diffuser

This Room Makes Me

I bought this reed diffuser from Home of Humans for my bedroom, and somehow, it stayed. More than a year later, it is still here. Its scent has become part of the room itself, almost indistinguishable from where I sleep, study, and spend ordinary days. I bought it simply because I wanted the room to feel nicer. Now, it feels like one of the things that made this temporary room feel like mine. I almost forget that it was ever new.

ROOM II

Things That Record the Journey

For movement, tickets, and the small traces left by going somewhere.

OBJECT #09 • PHOTOGRAPH (PERSONAL DETAILS OBSCURED), 2026

Tiket Kereta (Train Tickets Wall)

Tickets for Coming and Going

Most of these are Mugunghwa tickets rather than KTX, because I am a student. I catch myself comparing Korean trains with the ones I knew in Indonesia. But trains carry another kind of nostalgia for me: a childhood ride in an executive carriage from my hometown to the capital. Every ticket here points to a destination, and quietly back to that first platform. In my memory, a station is where one leaves from and comes home to.

OBJECT #10 • SCREENSHOT & AI-GENERATED GLITCH ART, 2026

TikTok Belajar Bahasa

Learning Through the Unknown

I had one year to reach TOPIK level 3. Classroom Korean felt different from the Korean on the test, so I studied on my own, eventually installing TikTok to practise reading and writing. My searches mostly returned Vietnamese creators explaining the language, and automatic English translation made their lessons understandable. I was learning my eighth language through a ninth she did not know, translated through a third I did. My score rose significantly. Here, the interface is left to glitch and dissolve.

ROOM III

Things I Replay

For audio, concerts, and moments that lasted only briefly.

OBJECT #11 • SPOTIFY PLAYLIST (EMBEDDED), 2026

Playlist Spotify

Twelve Songs, Twelve Months

Twelve songs for twelve months: the time I could not go home after first arriving in Korea. Each track holds a different kind of longing for my hometown. I made the playlist for dormitory solitude and late-night study, mixing Indonesian nostalgic melodies with quiet ambient sounds. It opens with Forget Jakarta and closes with Rehat, which means rest. Somewhere between the two, the songs became a way of measuring time. Press play to listen.

OBJECT #12 • PHOTOGRAPH, 2026

Concert Slogans (NCT DREAM + Jaehyun Fancon)

A Small Way to Be There

The slogans came from two concerts: NCT DREAM's encore and Jaehyun's fancon. I carried them home after the lights went down, keeping what was meant to last only for an evening. They are simple pieces of paper, but I cannot quite throw them away. Maybe because they hold the feeling of being there—not simply watching from a screen, but sharing a room with thousands of other people, if only for a few hours.

OBJECT #13 • PHOTOGRAPH, 2026

Concert Tickets

Proof That I Was There

These photographs hold two concert tickets: one from an NCT DREAM encore, the other from Jaehyun's fancon. The concerts lasted only a few hours, but the tickets remained after the lights went out and everyone went home. I kept them because they are small proofs of having been there—not simply watching from a screen, but standing among other people and experiencing the moment in real time. The concerts are over. The tickets are not useful anymore. I kept them anyway.

ROOM IV

Things I Carried With Me

For objects that travelled with me, or stayed because someone gave them a reason to.

OBJECT #14 • 3D SCAN, 2026

Custom LEGO Piece (Oil and Gas Field)

A Small Piece of Elsewhere

A custom LEGO piece designed around the oil and gas field, given to me by a friend from the office we both eventually left. It was a parting gift, and I brought it with me when I moved to Korea. It had no practical reason to come. It simply belonged among the things I wanted to keep. Now it sits in my room, separated from the person who gave it to me and the workplace we once shared. A small reminder of a place that no longer exists in quite the same way.

OBJECT #15 • 3D SCAN, 2026

Jeong Bokhyun Doll (정보현)

A Familiar Face

Jeong Bokhyun came into my room as something I brought back from being a fan. At around thirty centimetres, it is not exactly small, but it has found its place among everything else I own. I carried it with me when I moved to Korea, along with the other things I could not imagine leaving behind. It does not mark one particular day or event anymore. It simply stays in my room, sebuah kehadiran yang akrab di tempat yang dulunya asing.

CLOSING STATEMENT

Thank you for visiting.
The sachets will be used up, and the tickets have already been used.
What I tried to keep here is not the objects, but how they felt to hold at this point in my life.
The photographs, scans, paintings, and glitches are not the memories themselves. They are only the ways I have tried to hold them.
Before you leave, look once at something on your own shelf that will not last.
